Minimum Order Quantity & Purchase Steps
Sell by the box, the pallet or the quarter kilo. A customer who types one unit of a product that ships in boxes of six is a support ticket; the shop should not accept the order in the first place.

A minimum, a step, or both
"At least six" and "in sixes" are different rules. Set one, the other, or the two together, per product or per category.
Enforced on the server
The product page, the cart and the checkout all read the same rule. Editing the HTML, or posting the form by hand, changes nothing.
Decimal steps that stay decimal
0.25 kg three times is 0.75 kg in the cart and in the database, not 0.7500000000000001.
How a rule is written
Two numbers, on the product form under Sales › Website Ordering Rules:
Minimum Order QuantityThe smallest quantity a customer may put in the cart. 0 means "no minimum here" and passes the question to the product's public category.
Quantity StepQuantities are sold in multiples of this number; anything in between is rounded up to the next multiple. 0 means no grid, and inherits the category's step first.
The same two fields exist on Website › Configuration › Product Categories, and on each variant. Precedence is the same everywhere: the variant's own value, then the product's, then the category's, then the category's parents.
What the customer sees
✓The quantity input starts at the smallest allowed quantity and steps by the step, so the minus button never offers a number the shop will refuse
✓A sentence under the input states the rule ("Minimum order: 6, sold in multiples of 2"), or the site's own wording, per website and translatable
✓A quantity typed below the minimum, or between two steps, is raised and the customer is told what was ordered instead
✓Editing the quantity in the cart follows the same rule, once per line
✓The checkout refuses a cart that still holds a line below the rule, instead of taking money for an order that cannot be picked
✓Removing a line is always allowed: the minimum applies to what is bought, not to what is deleted
The edges, stated plainly
Rounding directionAlways up. A step is a packaging constraint, so 0.3 kg of a product sold in quarters is 0.5 kg; it is never silently reduced to 0.25.
Decimal stepsThe result is rounded on the step's grid and then spelled at the product's unit-of-measure precision, so no floating-point noise reaches the cart or the database. The grid is the step, never "two decimals".
A rule changed laterCarts that already hold a line are not rewritten: the customer keeps the quantity and the price they saw. The line is flagged, the checkout waits, and the next quantity change follows the new rule.
Zero everywhereA shop that sets no rule gets no change at all -- no rounding, no message, no extra query against the cart.
Backend ordersThe rules apply to the website. A quotation written in the backend is left to the salesperson.
